Consular Rules & Guidance

Understanding Your Schengen Visit Visa

Navigating the European Schengen Area requires understanding essential consular principles. NEXORA Travels and Tours provides transparent, accurate guidance based on official European Union visa frameworks.

Multi-Country Access

A Schengen short-stay visit visa (Type C) may permit eligible travelers to travel freely between 29 member states within the Schengen zone, subject to the visa's validity period, duration of stay (up to 90 days in any 180-day period), and entry requirements.

Key Principle: Seamless cross-border exploration across Europe without internal border checks.

The "Main Destination" Rule

Under Schengen visa regulations, you cannot simply apply to any embassy for convenience. For single-country visits, apply through that country. For trips involving multiple countries, you must submit your application to the country that constitutes your main destination in terms of length or purpose of stay.

Itinerary Based: If spending equal time in multiple countries, the country of first entry determines the competent consulate.

Tailored Application Circumstances

Documentation criteria, biometric appointments, and processing timelines vary according to each applicant's individual profile, travel purpose (tourism, family visit, business), employment background, and current consular jurisdiction in Sri Lanka.

Official Standards: Requirements adhere strictly to official European Commission and embassy directives.

Frequently Asked Questions

Clear answers to your important Schengen visit visa questions.

A Schengen short-stay visa (Type C) allows eligible third-country nationals to enter and stay within the 29 countries of the Schengen Area for up to 90 days within any 180-day rolling period. It is intended for tourism, family visits, business meetings, and short-term non-employment purposes.

NEXORA provides specialized assistance for nine premier destinations: Germany, Austria, Croatia, Malta, Norway, Sweden, Greece, Italy, and Spain, as well as general guidance for other Schengen member states.

Under Article 5 of the Schengen Visa Code, if visiting only one member state, you must apply to that state. If visiting multiple states, you must apply to the state where you will spend the longest time (main destination). If equal time is spent across countries, you must apply to the country of first entry into the Schengen zone.

Generally required documents include: a valid passport (valid at least 3 months beyond intended departure with 2 blank pages), completed application form, recent biometric passport photos, travel medical insurance (€30,000 coverage), flight itinerary, confirmed accommodation, proof of financial solvency (recent bank statements), and employment/business proof. Exact requirements depend on your specific purpose of visit.

Consulates accept applications up to 6 months before your intended departure date (9 months for seafarers), and no later than 15 calendar days before travel. We strongly advise initiating your file preparation at least 2 to 3 months in advance to secure appointment slots and allow ample processing time.
